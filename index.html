How the $FUEL amount is priced
The $FUEL amount follows its market price: if $FUEL drops 90%, an entry takes ten times as much of it. The price is the FUEL/WPLS PulseX pair's spot price, floored by a 15-minute average the auction builds from its own checkpoints of the pair: a drop is charged at once, a price pushed up for a few blocks does not make entries cheaper, and entries never pause for a price move. White paper

A 1,000-day daily auction on PulseChain (chain 369). Supply is hard-capped at 10,000,000 PAMP. Every entry is worth a flat 25,000 PLS: 50% of it is $FUEL, burned in the same transaction; 20% is PLS yield paid to PAMP that is unclaimed or staked; 30% feeds a PAMP buy-and-burn that drips a share of its pool a day. Claimed PAMP can be staked for 7 to 1,000 days.

How the auction works

The entry price is fixed and the token rate is not. Each day is a fixed 86,400-second window in which anyone may buy 1 to 1,000 entries per transaction. An entry is worth 25,000 PLS, paid partly as PLS with the transaction and partly as 50% of that value worth of $FUEL taken from the buyer's wallet. There is no limit on how many transactions one address sends.

When the day closes, that day's emission is split across every entry sold that day:

your PAMP  =  emission(d)  ×  your entries on d total entries on d

Everyone who enters on a given day receives the same rate, so there is nothing to gain by timing a purchase within the day. The rate falls as the day sells more: a day that sells 10 entries pays ten times per entry what the same day would pay at 100. A day that sells nothing mints nothing, which is why final supply can end below the cap but never above it.

Entering and claiming are separate steps. Entering records your share; after the day closes you call claim(day), or claimMany for several days at once, and the contract mints your PAMP then — together with the PLS yield that PAMP earned while it waited (see below). Unclaimed days do not expire.

The contract takes exactly the $FUEL an entry costs at the moment the transaction lands. The caller passes maxFuel, a cap on what may be taken, so a price move between quoting and landing cannot take more than the caller agreed to; the transaction reverts instead and nothing is spent.

Emission schedule

The first two weeks are steep: days 1 to 14 fall at 0.948 a day, so day 14 emits exactly half of day 1 (48,653 PAMP on day 1, 24,326 on day 14). From day 14 the Robinhood curve takes over — every day emits 0.997698× the day before — down to 2,507 PAMP on day 1,000, a tenth of day 14. The table also holds a day 0, one step above day 1 on the same slope, minted once at genesis. All 1,001 amounts sum to exactly 10,000,000 PAMP and are stored on chain as a fixed table whose sum the contract checks when it is deployed. The first fortnight of auctions hands out about 4.93% of the supply.

Where each entry goes

Every entry splits its value in the same transaction, in fixed proportions the contract stores as constants:

50% $FUEL, burned Half the entry's value in $FUEL, priced by the FUEL/WPLS PulseX pair, moved from the buyer's wallet straight to 0x…dEaD. It never sits in a contract.
20% PLS yield 5,000 PLS per entry into the yield contract, split at once over every PAMP that is unclaimed or staked.
30% Buy and burn $PAMP 7,500 PLS per entry into the burner's pool, which drips 1–15% a day (1% at launch) into PAMP buys sent to 0x…dEaD.

Pricing the $FUEL share

Each entry takes half its value in $FUEL. Converting that value into a $FUEL amount needs a price, and the price has to survive two things: a genuine fast move, and someone pushing the pool for a few blocks to make entries cheaper.

PulseX pairs are Uniswap V2 pairs: they publish a running cumulative of their price but keep no history, so the auction keeps its own. Every entry, and anyone who calls poke(), stores a checkpoint of the pair's cumulative price — at most one every 10 seconds, in a ring of 128. From those the contract reads the price two ways — spot, and the 15-minute average — and charges whichever gives more $FUEL per entry:

  • A drop is charged at once. If $FUEL falls 28% in one block, the next entry takes about 39% more $FUEL: spot already says so.
  • A pump buys no discount. If $FUEL is pushed up for a few blocks, spot says entries should be cheap, but the 15-minute average still applies.
  • Entries never pause for a price move. The only thing that stops pricing is having no checkpoint at least 15 minutes old, which is true only for the first 15 minutes after deployment. A collapse to a tenth of today's price simply means ten times the $FUEL per entry.

The contract also keeps a readable price log: spot, the average and the $FUEL per entry being charged, stored with each checkpoint once the average exists (samples, latestSample, samplesRange) and emitted as an event, so the price the auction charged at any moment is a matter of record.

A manipulation that did work would have to hold the pair's price for a full 15 minutes against anyone who trades it back, paying the pair's 0.29% fee and impact both ways, for a discount capped at the size of the move on 50% of the entries bought inside that window.

The PLS yield

A fifth of every entry is paid to PAMP that is not sitting in a wallet: PAMP still unclaimed in the auction, and PAMP locked in the staking contract. One index does the whole job. It counts the PLS ever distributed per PAMP of eligible weight, and every deposit raises it by the deposit divided by the weight of that moment:

index  +=  PLS deposited unclaimed PAMP + staked PAMP
  • Unclaimed PAMP. When a day closes, the auction records the index at that moment. Claiming the day later pays amount × (index now − index at close) in PLS along with the PAMP. Leave a day unclaimed for a year and it earns for a year.
  • Staked PAMP. A stake records the index when it opens. claimPls pays what it has earned since and leaves the stake open; unstake pays it in full, whatever the PAMP penalty. PLS already earned is never reduced.
  • Wallet PAMP earns nothing. Between a claim and a stake, tokens are out of the index. Claim and stake in one go to keep them in it.
  • Nothing is lost. A deposit that arrives while nothing is eligible waits, and rounding remainders wait too; both join the next split. Only the auction and the staking contract can take PLS out, each for exactly what it has computed.

The $PAMP buy and burn

Thirty percent of every entry lands in a pool that buys PAMP and sends it to 0x…dEaD. The contract is a port of the $FUEL protocol's own buy-and-burn (itself a cut-down LessBuyAndProcess), with the same drip:

  • A share of the pool every day — 1% at launch, adjustable between 1% and 15% by the rate setter — in 144 intervals of 10 minutes. Each interval is entitled to 1/144 of the day's allocation, computed from the pool as it stands when the interval opens; intervals nobody executes stack onto the next call, capped at the pool.
  • Anyone executes it with swapPlsForPampAndBurn and keeps 1.5% of the interval's PLS. The rest is wrapped, sent to the PAMP/WPLS PulseX pair, and the PAMP the pair pays goes straight to the dead address.
  • A 15-minute average guards the swap. The burner keeps its own checkpoints of the pair, like the auction does for $FUEL, and refuses any swap that would deliver less than 90% of the average's quote. A thin or pushed pool parks the interval rather than buying badly.
  • Deposits never touch the interval bookkeeping. Only the burn function advances it, which is the fix the $FUEL burner shipped after community testing found two back-to-back deposits could orphan an interval.

What the original left to an owner — the daily rate (1–10%) and the slippage tolerance — is narrowed here: a rate setter fixed at deployment may move the daily rate between 1% and 15% (1% at launch), and the slippage tolerance is a constant 10%. That rate is the only adjustable parameter in PAMP; nothing else has an owner. The original's pool creation, liquidity seeding and fee collection are gone too: the PAMP/WPLS pair is seeded by hand, and until it exists and has 15 minutes of history the burner reports "not started yet" and the PLS simply accumulates.

Staking

Claimed PAMP can be locked in a staking contract that is the Robinhood Chain PAMP staking contract — itself a fork of the $MORE staking contract, taken from Sourcify — with PLS rewards added. The mechanics are the original's; the token is $PAMP.

  • Lock 7 to 1,000 days. Any amount; several stakes per address.
  • Leaving early forfeits the unserved share. A stake at 25% of its term keeps 25% of principal plus PAMP rewards; the other 75% is the penalty.
  • 14 days of grace after unlock, then a late penalty grows linearly and reaches 100% a further 700 days on.
  • Every PAMP penalty is recycled to the stakes still open, pro rata by amount, through a cumulative reward-per-token index. That is the only source of PAMP rewards: no emission, no fixed rate.
  • PLS is separate. Every open stake earns the protocol's PLS yield from the index above; penalties never touch it.

There is no owner. The original's one owner power — sweeping tokens sent to the contract by mistake — is permissionless here and can never touch $PAMP. Because $PAMP supports EIP-2612, stakeWithPermit opens a stake in one transaction.

Parameters

Six contracts on PulseChain (chain 369): the emission table, the token, the yield, the buy-and-burn, the auction and the staking. Every parameter below is a constant or immutable. None can be changed after deployment, by anyone. Contract addresses are published on the dashboard, which reads each one back from the chain and flags any mismatch.

There is no admin role, no pause and no upgrade path in any of the six contracts, and no owner apart from the burner's rate setter, a fixed address whose only power is the daily drip rate. The token's configure function, which names the auction as the only minter, is callable by anyone exactly once and is called in the deployment transaction sequence. The yield contract's two spenders are fixed at deployment from predicted addresses and asserted afterwards. Every mechanism is covered by unit tests against a mock PulseX pair that enforces the real 0.29% fee, and by fork tests against the live chain that buy from a real pair, seed a real PAMP/WPLS pair and run a real burn. A read-only verification script audits the live deployment and reverts on the first mismatch.
